# 05.5 — Fallback Sinks

## Intent

When Home Assistant is unreachable, alerts must still land somewhere you'll see them — ntfy,
Telegram, or email. Same `AlertSink` interface (05.4), different transports. A chain-of-sinks
composes them: try primary, on exhaustion try the next. This is what keeps a HA outage from
silently swallowing a good deal.

## Contract

```scala
final class ChainSink[F[_]](primary: AlertSink[F], fallbacks: List[AlertSink[F]]) extends AlertSink[F]
final class NtfySink[F[_]](cfg: NtfyConfig) extends AlertSink[F]
final class EmailSink[F[_]](cfg: SmtpConfig) extends AlertSink[F]
```

- The chain delivers to the **first** sink that succeeds; it does not fan out to all (you
  don't want the same alert on three channels). Order is config.
- Every sink is opt-in and configured by you. No sink targets anything derived from flyer
  data.
- A total failure across all sinks is logged and surfaced as a run-health signal (08.3), not
  swallowed.

## Gherkin

```gherkin
Feature: Fallback delivery chain

  @boundary
  Scenario: The chain stops at the first successful sink
    Given a chain of [HA, ntfy, email]
    And HA succeeds
    When I deliver an alert
    Then only HA is called

  @boundary
  Scenario: The chain advances past a failing sink
    Given a chain of [HA, ntfy, email]
    And HA fails after retries
    And ntfy succeeds
    When I deliver an alert
    Then ntfy delivers it
    And email is not called

  @boundary
  Scenario: Total failure is surfaced, not swallowed
    Given every sink in the chain fails
    When I deliver an alert
    Then the failure is recorded as a run-health signal
    And the error identifies which sinks were attempted
