# 07.3 — Deal Quality Scorer

## Intent

Turn stats + the current observation + any enrichment into a human `DealVerdict` — the phrase
that makes an alert worth reading ("cheapest in 8 weeks", "20% below usual", "not actually a
deal"). This is what 05.1 consults for `requireSale`/`minDiscountPct` and what 05.3 renders.
Pure function; the judgement, not the notification.

## Verdict model

```scala
enum DealVerdict:
  case BestEver(sinceWeeks: Int)
  case BelowUsual(pctBelowMedian: Int)
  case Notable                       // a real sale, modest
  case AtOrAboveUsual                // not a deal, even if "on sale"
  case Unknown                       // insufficient history / no price

def scoreDeal(obs: PriceObservation, stats: PriceStats,
              enrichment: Option[EnrichedPrice]): DealVerdict
```

Rule sketch (pinned, tunable via config thresholds):
- No effective price and no enrichment → `Unknown`.
- Effective price ≤ trailing min → `BestEver(window weeks)`.
- Effective price ≥ configurable pct below weighted median → `BelowUsual(pct)`.
- A struck-through "regular" that enrichment contradicts (CT case, 06.4) → discount computed
  against the *enrichment* regular, not the flyer's claim.
- Price at/above weighted median → `AtOrAboveUsual` (this is the false-sale detector; a
  flyer can shout "SALE" on a normal price).
- Thin history (n below a floor) → `Notable` at most, never `BestEver` (don't over-claim on
  two data points).

**AMENDED 2026-08-20 (from a live run).** Two corrections, both found by running this
against 17,881 real observations:

- The observation being scored must be EXCLUDED from the history it is scored against.
  `observationsFor` returns every row for a product key, including the one under
  judgement. On a first run, where 16,827 of 17,293 products had exactly one
  observation, each item became its own median and 370 of 384 alerts came back
  "not actually a deal".
- Thin history caps the NEGATIVE side too, not just the optimistic one. Below the
  minimum-n floor the verdict is `Unknown`, not `AtOrAboveUsual` — one or two
  observations cannot support "this is not a deal" any more than they can support
  "this is the cheapest ever". Enrichment is the exception: a retailer's actual regular
  price is a real baseline however little history exists.

The second matters most in combination with `requireSale` (05.1), which is now on by
default. A confident false negative there suppresses every alert for the weeks it takes
history to accumulate, while the service looks perfectly healthy.
